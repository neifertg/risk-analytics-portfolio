# UCSB Audit of Third Party IT Services (2014)

## What This Document Is

A real, published UC Santa Barbara Audit and Advisory Services report
(Audit Report No. 08-14-0014, August 1, 2014), available at
[auditreports.ucop.edu](https://auditreports.ucop.edu/?action=public_ar_display&id=e3acb008a055727f).
Written in 2014, when cloud/third-party IT adoption on campus was
accelerating faster than campus policy had caught up — a real,
time-stamped snapshot of an institution recognizing a governance gap
early, rather than a generic, timeless description of cloud risk.

## Scope

The audit tested whether UCSB had adequate controls over — and
monitoring of — decentralized departments' "casual" use of third-party IT
services (file hosting, cloud infrastructure, SaaS tools) obtained
without involvement from central IT or Procurement Services, often
because the services were free or paid via a small-purchase card rather
than through a normal procurement process.

## Findings

The audit's summary opinion was direct: **existing policies, procedures,
and guidelines were not sufficient** to ensure protection of University
data and compliance with laws, regulations, and UC policy for this kind
of casual third-party IT use. The report recommended the campus develop,
vet, and issue a dedicated policy covering purchase and use of
third-party IT services, plus more detailed procedural guidance — and
noted that some other UC campuses had already implemented and
communicated relevant guidelines their own campus community could learn
from. This is a real example of exactly the gap this corpus's synthetic
[Cloud Computing Controls Testing](../../corpus/cloud-computing-controls-testing.md)
procedure describes in the abstract: decentralized, unmanaged cloud
adoption outrunning the policy meant to govern it.

## Why This Matters for the Tailored-vs-Generic Contrast

The generic corpus's cloud-controls procedure tells an auditor *what to
test for* in cloud governance generally. This real report shows what that
gap actually looked like at one real institution, at a specific point in
time, with a specific management response — a level of "here's what
actually happened" grounding no synthetic procedure can offer, however
well-written.
