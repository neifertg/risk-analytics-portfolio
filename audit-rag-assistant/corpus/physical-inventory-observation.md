# Physical Inventory Observation

## Objective

Obtain firsthand evidence that inventory recorded in the perpetual system
actually exists, in the condition and quantity recorded, by observing the
client's physical count and independently test-counting a sample rather
than relying solely on the client's count sheets.

## Pre-Count Planning

Before the count date, confirm the count instructions issued to count
teams cover cutoff procedures (how receiving and shipping are handled
during the count), how count teams are assigned to avoid a team counting
an area they normally manage, and how obsolete, damaged, or slow-moving
inventory is identified and tagged separately during the count rather than
counted at full value alongside good inventory.

## Observing the Count

During the count, observe whether count teams are actually following the
issued instructions — proper two-person count-and-recount teams, tags
sequentially controlled so none are lost or duplicated, and no
uncontrolled movement of inventory in or out of the counted area while
counting is in progress. Independently test-count a risk-based sample of
items (high-dollar items, items with prior-period discrepancies, and a
selection from each major location) and compare test counts against both
the client's count sheets and the perpetual system quantity on hand.

## Cutoff and Reconciliation

Record the last shipping and receiving document numbers used before the
count and the first ones used after, so cutoff can be tested later against
the recorded sale or purchase — inventory in transit at the count date is
a common source of double-counting or omission if cutoff isn't pinned down
precisely. After the count, obtain the client's reconciliation of count
results to the perpetual system and evaluate whether the resulting
adjustment is consistent with the discrepancies actually observed during
the count, not just accepted as given.
