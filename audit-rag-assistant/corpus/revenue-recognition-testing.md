# Revenue Recognition Testing

## Objective

Confirm revenue recognized in the period reflects performance obligations
that were actually satisfied — goods delivered, services rendered, or
milestones met per the contract terms — rather than revenue pulled
forward or pushed back to hit a period's targets.

## Cutoff Testing

Sample transactions recorded in the days immediately before and after
period-end and trace each to underlying shipping documents, service
completion records, or system-of-record timestamps to confirm the
recognition date matches when the performance obligation was actually
satisfied, not when the invoice was generated or payment received.
Cutoff errors cluster right around period-end almost by definition, so
testing a window that straddles the cutoff date is far more
effective than sampling uniformly across the whole period.

## Multi-Element Arrangement Allocation

For contracts bundling multiple performance obligations (e.g. hardware,
implementation services, and a support subscription in one deal), test
that the total contract value was allocated across obligations based on
each element's standalone selling price, and that revenue for each
element was recognized only as that specific obligation was satisfied —
not all at contract signing. Look specifically for arrangements where a
disproportionate share of the total price was allocated to the element
recognized earliest, since that pattern can indicate revenue being
accelerated through allocation rather than genuine timing.

## Credit Memo and Reversal Review

Review credit memos and revenue reversals issued shortly after period-end
close, since a pattern of reversals concentrated right after close can
indicate revenue was recognized prematurely and then walked back once the
period was already reported. A credit memo tied to a genuine return or
service issue is normal; a cluster of reversals against large, late-
period transactions from the prior period warrants follow-up.
