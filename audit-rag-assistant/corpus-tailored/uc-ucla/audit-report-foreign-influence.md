# UC Systemwide Foreign Influence Audit (2021)

## What This Document Is

A real, published UC systemwide audit report (Project P20A004, February
2021), available at
[auditreports.ucop.edu](https://auditreports.ucop.edu/?action=public_ar_display&id=c2bb40b7ca7dc9f2)
— one of over 2,600 real completed audit reports UC publishes publicly.
This audit examined a risk area — foreign influence on research and
academic activity — that doesn't have a dedicated procedure anywhere in
this demo's synthetic corpus, since it's a specific higher-education/
research-institution risk rather than a generic business-audit topic.
That gap is itself informative: a generic audit-methodology corpus can't
anticipate every sector-specific risk a real organization actually faces.

## Scope

The audit covered controls across nine areas tied to foreign-influence
risk at UC's research-intensive campuses: disclosure of foreign
affiliations and financial interests by faculty and researchers,
conflict-of-interest review processes, export-control compliance for
controlled technology and information, and restricted-party screening
before entering into foreign collaborations or accepting foreign funding.
This mirrors, at a real-institution level of specificity, the kind of
compliance-control assurance work this corpus's synthetic
[Third-Party Vendor Risk Management](../../corpus/third-party-vendor-risk-management.md)
and
[Data Privacy Compliance Testing](../../corpus/data-privacy-compliance-testing.md)
procedures describe generically for other compliance domains.

## Findings

The audit identified real gaps across several of its nine scope areas —
including inconsistencies in how foreign-affiliation disclosures were
collected and reviewed across UC's multiple locations, and opportunities
to strengthen restricted-party screening before foreign engagements were
finalized. Each finding carries a documented management corrective action
with an assigned owner and target timeline, following the same
observation → recommendation → management-response structure this
corpus's synthetic audit-report style guidance implies but that this
document demonstrates for real, at systemwide scale, across nine distinct
risk areas in one engagement.

## Why This Matters for the Tailored-vs-Generic Contrast

A generic audit assistant asked "what should we test for foreign
influence risk" has nothing in this demo's synthetic corpus to draw on —
it isn't a topic area the synthetic procedures cover. An assistant
grounded in this real report can instead point to what UC's own internal
audit function actually found and recommended, in a real institutional
context, which is precisely the value proposition of adding a tailored
corpus on top of generic methodology: the tailored layer covers risks and
specifics the generic layer was never built to anticipate.
