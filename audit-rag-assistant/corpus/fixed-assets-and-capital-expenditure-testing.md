# Fixed Assets and Capital Expenditure Testing

## Scope

Covers capital expenditure (CapEx) approval, the capitalize-versus-expense
judgment applied to qualifying costs, and physical verification of assets
already on the fixed-asset register. Purchase-order matching mechanics for
the underlying vendor payment are covered in [Procurement and Purchase
Order Matching Controls](procurement-and-purchase-order-matching-controls.md);
this procedure picks up at the capital-specific questions that ordinary
purchasing controls don't address.

## Capital Expenditure Authorization

Sample capital projects initiated during the period and confirm each was
approved at the authority level required by its dollar amount under the
delegation-of-authority matrix before any binding commitment was made —
a signed contract, an issued purchase order, or a vendor deposit. A
project split into multiple smaller requests that individually fall under
an approval threshold, but that were known in advance to be part of one
larger initiative, is a finding regardless of whether each individual
piece was properly approved at its own (understated) level — the control
is testing whether the true scope of commitment received the right level
of scrutiny, not whether each artificially-divided piece cleared its own
bar.

## Capitalization Threshold and Classification Testing

Sample costs recorded as capital and costs recorded as expense around the
capitalization threshold and confirm each was classified per the written
accounting policy — asset useful life, threshold dollar amount, and
whether the cost represents an improvement (capitalizable) versus a
repair or maintenance (expensed). Costs clustered just above or just below
the threshold deserve particular attention, since that's the range where
a preparer under pressure to hit a budget or earnings target has the
clearest incentive to misclassify in either direction.

## Physical Verification and Impairment Indicators

Select a sample of assets from the fixed-asset register — weighted toward
higher-value and higher-risk categories such as mobile or easily
disposed-of equipment — and confirm physical existence, location, and
condition against the register. For assets not located, distinguish
between a recordkeeping error (asset exists but the register is wrong)
and an actual loss requiring write-off and, if the pattern suggests it,
a fraud referral. Separately, note any assets showing indicators of
impairment (idle, damaged, obsolete, or associated with a discontinued
process) that haven't been evaluated for a potential write-down, since an
asset carried at full value despite a clear impairment indicator overstates
the balance sheet regardless of whether it's also physically present.
