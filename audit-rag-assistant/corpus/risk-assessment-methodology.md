# Audit Risk Assessment Methodology

## The Audit Risk Model

Audit risk is commonly decomposed into inherent risk (how susceptible an
area is to misstatement or failure before considering any controls),
control risk (how likely existing controls are to fail to prevent or
detect that misstatement), and detection risk (the risk that the audit's
own procedures fail to catch a misstatement that controls didn't). The
relationship between the three drives how much testing an area needs:
high inherent and control risk calls for lower acceptable detection risk,
which means more extensive testing.

## Building the Auditable Universe Risk Ranking

The annual audit plan starts from a full inventory of auditable entities
(processes, systems, business units, entities) scored against consistent
risk factors — financial significance, complexity, regulatory exposure,
history of prior findings, time since last audit, and management's own
risk assessment input. Areas scoring highest get planned first and get
more hours; a low-risk area with no history of issues may go several
years between audits, while a high-risk area gets audited annually or
more often.

## Refreshing the Assessment Mid-Cycle

Risk rankings aren't static for the life of the annual plan — a
significant system change, a new regulatory requirement, a major
personnel turnover in a key process, or an emerging fraud pattern
elsewhere in the industry should trigger a re-ranking of the affected
area, potentially moving up a planned audit or adding an unplanned one.
Treat the annual risk assessment as the starting point for the plan, not
a fixed commitment that ignores what's actually happened since it was
built.
