# UCLA Systemwide Audit of Undergraduate Admissions (2019)

## What This Document Is

A real, published UCLA Audit & Advisory Services report (#19-714007,
November 2019), available at
[auditreports.ucop.edu](https://auditreports.ucop.edu/?action=public_ar_display&id=c429efd704963bdc).
This engagement was performed shortly after the 2019 "Varsity Blues"
national college-admissions fraud scandal, which involved falsified
special-talent (notably athletic) admissions at several universities —
real, dated context a synthetic corpus can't replicate, since it requires
knowing what was actually happening in the real world when the audit was
scoped.

## Scope

The audit examined UCLA's undergraduate admissions process specifically
around special-talent admissions — cases where an applicant's admission
depends partly on a non-academic talent (most sensitively, recruited
athletics) rather than academic criteria alone — testing whether the
verification and approval process was independent, documented, and
resistant to the kind of manipulation the Varsity Blues cases exploited
nationally.

## Findings and Recommendations

The audit's real, specific recommendations included: implementing
two-step verification for special-talent admissions decisions (so no
single reviewer's judgment alone determines an admit), and establishing
documented minimum-verification requirements that a candidate's claimed
talent must actually satisfy before it factors into an admissions
decision. Each recommendation carries a campus management response with
an assigned target date — the real accountability structure this corpus's
synthetic
[Audit Committee Reporting and Issue Tracking](../../corpus/audit-committee-reporting-and-issue-tracking.md)
procedure describes only in general terms ("findings should be
independently validated as remediated," "track to closure with an owner
and date").
