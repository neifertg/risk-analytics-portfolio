# Audit Committee Reporting and Issue Tracking

## Standard Reporting Content

Each cycle's audit committee report should include: audit plan status
against the annual plan (completed, in progress, delayed and why), a
summary of new findings by severity, the status of previously reported
open findings including any past their original due date, and any
scope limitations or management disagreements encountered during the
period. A report that only lists new findings without reconciling
against the prior period's open-issue population lets aging findings
quietly disappear from visibility.

## Finding Severity and Escalation Consistency

Confirm findings are rated against a consistent severity definition
(e.g. low/moderate/high/critical tied to financial, operational, or
compliance impact) regardless of which auditor or engagement identified
them — severity ratings that vary by who wrote the finding rather than
its actual risk undermine the aggregate reporting the audit committee
relies on. Critical and high findings should have a defined maximum time
before they're escalated to the audit committee outside the normal
reporting cadence, not held until the next scheduled meeting.

## Remediation Validation, Not Self-Reported Closure

Before closing a finding as remediated, confirm internal audit
independently validated the corrective action actually occurred and
addresses the root cause — re-performing the original test procedure on
a current sample, not just accepting management's written attestation
that the issue is fixed. A finding closed on attestation alone that
recurs in the next audit cycle is a strong signal the validation step is
being skipped.

## Aging and Overdue Finding Reporting

Track each open finding's age against its committed remediation date and
report findings past that date as explicitly overdue, with the reason
for delay and a revised date — not folded silently back into the general
open-findings count as if nothing had changed. A pattern of repeatedly
revised due dates for the same finding, without escalation, indicates
the remediation-tracking process itself needs review.
