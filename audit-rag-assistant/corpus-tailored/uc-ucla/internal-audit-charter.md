# University of California: Internal Audit Charter

## What This Document Is

This is the real, currently-published Internal Audit Charter for the
University of California system, maintained by Ethics, Compliance and
Audit Services (ECAS) and available at
[ucop.edu/ethics-compliance-audit-services/audit/internal-audit-charter.html](https://www.ucop.edu/ethics-compliance-audit-services/audit/internal-audit-charter.html).
Unlike the generic
[Audit Risk Assessment Methodology](../../corpus/risk-assessment-methodology.md)
procedure in this demo's synthetic corpus, this document is UC's actual
governing charter, summarized here rather than reproduced verbatim.

## Reporting Structure and Independence

The Chief Compliance and Audit Officer (CCAO) reports on a dual-reporting
basis: functionally to the Regents (through the Board's Compliance and
Audit Committee) and administratively to the President. This dual line is
the structural mechanism for independence — the CCAO's evaluations,
resourcing, and continued tenure aren't controlled solely by the
management whose activities Internal Audit reviews. Individual UC
locations (each campus, each medical center, the Office of the President
itself, and affiliated units like Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory)
maintain their own local internal audit departments, which operate under
this same systemwide charter rather than each writing an independent one.

## Scope and Authority

The charter grants Internal Audit unrestricted access to records,
personnel, and physical properties relevant to any engagement, and states
that Internal Audit's scope covers evaluating and improving the
effectiveness of governance, risk management, and control processes
across the entire university system — not just financial controls.
Individual locations' local audit plans (see [Internal Audit Plan for
2022-23](annual-audit-plan-2022-23.md)) are built under this systemwide
charter's authority, alongside "systemwide audits" ECAS itself
coordinates across multiple locations at once.

## Standards Conformance

The charter commits the function to conformance with the IIA's Global
Internal Audit Standards — the same real standard summarized in this
wiki's private companion research (not part of this public demo corpus).
That conformance commitment is what makes UC's own charter a genuine,
citable example of a real organization operationalizing the abstract
"independence," "objectivity," and "board oversight" language this
corpus's own governance-adjacent notes discuss only in the generic.
