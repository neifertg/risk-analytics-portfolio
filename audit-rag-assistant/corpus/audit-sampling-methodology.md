# Audit Sampling Methodology

## Why Sample at All

Testing every transaction in a population is rarely feasible or
necessary — sampling lets the audit draw a conclusion about the whole
population from testing a subset, provided the sample is selected and
sized in a way that supports that inference. The two questions every
sampling plan has to answer are: which items get selected, and how many.

## Stage 1: Risk Stratification

Before selecting any items, split the population into strata by risk
characteristics — dollar value (e.g. above/below a materiality-linked
threshold), transaction complexity (manual journal entries vs. system-
generated), and known risk factors (prior-year exceptions, related-party
transactions, override of automated controls). High-risk strata get
tested more thoroughly than low-risk strata; this is what lets the audit
concentrate effort where a misstatement is more likely, rather than
spreading equal attention across a population where most of the risk sits
in a small fraction of the items.

## Stage 2: Sample Selection Within Strata

Within each stratum, select the actual sample using either statistical
methods (random sampling with a calculated sample size based on
confidence level, tolerable error, and expected error rate) or judgmental
selection (targeting specific high-risk items directly — large dollar
items, unusual account combinations, items flagged by data analytics).
Statistical sampling supports a quantified conclusion about the whole
stratum; judgmental sampling is faster and often used for the highest-risk
items where 100% coverage of the largest few transactions is more
efficient than a statistical draw.

## Evaluating Results

If a sample turns up exceptions, project the error rate back to the
stratum's population value (for statistical samples) and assess whether
the projected misstatement, combined with other findings, could exceed
performance materiality. A single exception in a small statistical sample
can imply a large potential population error — don't dismiss it without
running the projection.
